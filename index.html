One Ticket Service: what it would take

Consolidating Kapture, Ticket Service Java and SRS into a single governing service. This page is the build inventory — what gets retired, what gets built, and what has to change around it. Effort and sequencing are sized separately.

Ashish Raj · PM, Service Tickets POD
01

The change

Today — five hops, three systems of record
Ticket creation→ Kapture→ Ticket Service Java→ SRS→ TAS→ CSP app
Proposed — one governing body
Ticket creation→ New Ticket Service→ TAS→ CSP app

Scope: every ticket type, not only service. Kapture today spans ten categories and 55 dispositions — internet, payment, refund, cash collection, router pickup, shifting, change requests, connection removal and partner misbehaviour. SRS currently sees only a subset. The new service takes all of them.

The call-centre team gets a ticket-viewing and creation tool built once on an existing internal platform, reading and writing the same store — rather than a third-party CRM holding its own copy.

02

Why consolidate

What the customer runs into today

Every one of these is a consequence of the split — not a feature we forgot to build.

Told one deadline, held to anotherThe time promised to the customer is computed by one system; the CSP works to a different one
Calls their CSP and reaches a strangerThe app reads the partner's name and number from a legacy table, not the live record
Says it is working, and a CSP still turns upA closure made by an agent never reaches the CSP's app, so the visit still happens
Tells us when they are home, and we arrive laterAvailability is logged as a comment in one system that no other system reads
Repeats the whole story on a reopenThe link back to the original ticket is stored, but never sent to the CSP working it
Can see nothing, so calls againThe app reads a record that knows nothing about the work actually in progress

What three services cost us today

  • Three state machines. CLOSED exists in two of them and means different things — in SRS it means the Quality signal was sent, not that the work is done
  • Three databases, none authoritative
  • Three sets of backend logic that have gone unchecked against each other
  • The systems do not talk effectively — data stops at boundaries nobody owns
  • Every new person has to learn all three before changing anything

What one service gives us

  • One store. Agent view, CSP app and customer app all read the same record
  • One logic engine — reopening, TAT and classification defined once
  • One state machine. CLOSED means one thing, to every reader
  • No sync layer, so no sync failures to detect, reconcile or explain
  • One identity — CSP and customer profiles read from our systems, not re-created

The risk worth naming now. Ticket Service Java is the largest of the three, the oldest, and carries almost no automated tests — very little records what its correct behaviour actually is. Rebuilding it means re-deriving business rules from source, with no safety net telling us when one is wrong. Behaviour extraction has to be funded work, not an assumption.

04

What needs to be built

Eight blocks. Three are new software, three are changes to things that already exist, and two are the operational work of getting there safely.

B1

New Ticket Service — the core

Build

One service holding the ticket record, its state machine and every rule currently split across SRS and Ticket Service Java.

Ticket domain + single state machineOne vocabulary replacing four; every transition defined once
One TAT / SLA enginePer-subtype deadlines on one clock, with one working-window definition
Classification & routingIssue type → subtype → executor, from one mapping table
Reopen lineage & chain scoringA reopen linked to its original, measured and scored as one problem
Verification & closureA check that can block a closure, with the customer as fallback
Escalation & timersFirst-response and breach handling, one ladder
Idempotency & dedupOne contact produces one ticket, however it arrives
Event publishingOutbound contracts to TAS, Quality OS and both apps
All ten ticket categoriesNot only service — payment, refund, cash collection, shifting, removal and the rest
Observability & alertingNeeded from day one; during dual-run it is the only way divergence is visible

What consolidation costs us. Today if SRS is down, agents still create tickets in Kapture and the record survives. Afterwards one service down means no tickets at all — no creation, no agent view, no CSP card. We are trading four fragile systems for one critical one, and it needs an availability target to match.

B3

Agent console

Build

The replacement for what Kapture does for the CX team — a ticket-viewing and creation surface reading and writing our own data. A one-time build on an existing internal tool (Wiom Hub or whichever platform we already run), not a new product. It is still the largest single new build here, and the one most likely to be underestimated.

Ticket list, search, filtersThe agent's daily working surface
Ticket detail + full historyOne timeline including CSP and technician activity
Create & updateDisposition capture, comments that reach the CSP
Queue & assignmentWhatever queue logic we choose to keep
CX reportingWhatever the team runs in Kapture today and still needs
IdentityAgents, CSPs and customers read from our systems — no duplicate profiles
All ten categoriesEvery workflow Kapture carries today, not just service tickets
Roles & access controlAgent permissions Kapture enforces today have to exist here

Change

B6

Data migration

Build

Eleven-plus stores collapse into one, without losing history or dropping tickets that are open at the moment of cutover.

Schema & state mappingFour status vocabularies reconciled to one, record by record
Historical backfillLast six months migrate in full; older records archive-only
In-flight cutoverHundreds of tickets are open at any moment; none can be stranded
ReconciliationProving old and new agree before we trust the new one
B7

Rollout

Change

Phased by CSP, with both systems live until the new one is proven.

Dual-runBoth systems processing, differences surfaced rather than discovered
CSP-wise phasingA controlled cohort before general rollout
CX trainingAgents move off Kapture — owned by the CX team, sequenced ahead of each phase
Reconciliation dashboardsThe measure that decides whether we proceed
Rollback pathReal, not nominal — once agents write to the new store, going back means reverse migration
B8

Decommission

Retire

The saving and the simplification only land when the old systems are actually switched off — not when the new one goes live.

Kapture contract exitWhere the licence cost stops
Ticket Service Java shutdownOnly after every consumer is repointed
SRS shutdownIncluding its timers and scheduled work
Archive & retentionHistorical access preserved wherever CX or finance needs it
